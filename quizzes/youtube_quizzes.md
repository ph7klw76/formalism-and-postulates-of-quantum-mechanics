# Original formative YouTube quiz entries

Enter manually where the channel supports native quizzes. These are not the QMFPS instrument.

## 01:13 / Q1

For the spin degree of freedom of a spin-½ system, what is the complex Hilbert-space dimension?

A. Two.
B. Three, because there are three spatial axes.
C. Infinite, even when all other degrees of freedom are excluded.

Answer: A. Two independent spin states span the spin-only space. Different measurement axes select different bases in that same space.

## 02:53 / Q2

Prepare |x+⟩ and measure S_z once. Which results can the ideal detector report?

A. Zero, because the expectation is zero.
B. Any angular momentum between −ħ/2 and +ħ/2.
C. Either +ħ/2 or −ħ/2.

Answer: C. A single ideal projective measurement yields an eigenvalue. In |x+⟩ these two outcomes each have probability 1/2; zero is their expectation, not a detector outcome.

## 04:30 / Q3

After recording x+ in an ideal S_x measurement, what is P(z+) in an immediate S_z measurement?

A. The original preparation probability, 0.6.
B. 1/2.
C. 1.

Answer: B. Condition on the recorded first result: the new state is |x+⟩. Its overlap squared with |z+⟩ is 1/2.

## 06:12 / Q4

The theory gives ⟨S_z⟩ = 0.1ħ and P(z+) = 0.6. What does that mean?

A. The probability-weighted theoretical mean is 0.1ħ; finite samples fluctuate.
B. Each detector result equals 0.1ħ.
C. Every batch of 1000 contains exactly 600 plus results.

Answer: A. The mean is an ensemble prediction. Individual outcomes are ±ħ/2 and the number of plus results in independent trials is binomial.

## 07:54 / Q5

Prepare |z+⟩, measure S_x and discard its result, then measure S_z. What is P(z+)?

A. 1, because ignoring the record means nothing changed.
B. 0, because incompatible measurements forbid z+.
C. 1/2.

Answer: C. The unrecorded ideal S_x measurement leaves an equal mixture of |x+⟩ and |x−⟩. Each gives P(z+) = 1/2.

## 09:39 / Q6

Start in |x+⟩ and evolve with H = ħωσ_z/2 through ωt = π. What is P(x+)?

A. 0.
B. 1/2.
C. 1.

Answer: A. The relative phase changes |x+⟩ into |x−⟩ up to an overall phase. Thus P(x+) = cos²(π/2) = 0, while both z probabilities remain 1/2.

## 11:27 / Q7

Two pure preparations have identical z-spin probabilities. Must all measurement predictions agree?

A. Yes; the z probabilities completely specify every pure spin state.
B. No; different relative phases can be distinguished in other bases.
C. Yes; normalization uniquely determines the relative phase.

Answer: B. One basis fixes populations but leaves relative phase undetermined. Additional x/y statistics can distinguish those states; include uncertainty and preparation assumptions.
